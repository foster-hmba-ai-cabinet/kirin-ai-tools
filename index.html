Ground Rules

Six principles to read before using AI on graded work.
+

How we use AI reflects on the program. These principles sit behind the Governance category above.

  1. Course policy wins. Rules differ by course and assignment. If a syllabus is silent, ask. Don't assume permission.
  2. Disclose, disclose, disclose. Undisclosed use carries the heaviest penalties; proactive disclosure, the lightest.
  3. Never paste confidential or personal data into a consumer AI tool: no employer data, client info, NDAs, or PII.
  4. AI is a starting point, not a source. Verify every fact, number, and citation. Models invent plausible references.
  5. Get consent before recording people. Transcription and voice tools capture others, so tell the room first.
  6. Beating a detector is still misconduct. Detectors are imperfect; disclosure is the real protection, not obfuscation.
What "course policy wins" looks like

Example: a no-AI course

Some Foster courses prohibit generative AI on graded work entirely. A representative policy: any use of tools like ChatGPT, Copilot, or Gemini on assignments, exams, or other graded activities is treated as academic misconduct and investigated; all submitted work must be the student's own.

The reasoning instructors give: assignments are designed to build creativity, critical thinking, and problem-solving, and leaning on AI short-circuits those learning goals.

The takeaway: policies differ from course to course, and conforming to each one is the student's responsibility. Everything on this site is fair game only where the specific course allows it. When unsure, ask the instructor.
